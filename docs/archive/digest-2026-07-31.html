CISA confirms active exploitation of hard-coded credential flaw in Cisco Firewall Management Center

↪ Distinct from the active Cisco SD-WAN zero-day campaign already on your radar (no new disclosures there today) — this is a separate FMC advisory worth tracking on its own.

CISA added CVE-2026-20316 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on July 29 after Cisco confirmed real-world attacks against Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) software. The flaw is a built-in, low-privileged account with a static, hard-coded password that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker log in and pull sensitive data; Cisco rates it “High” severity despite a 5.3 CVSS score because it can be chained with other FMC bugs to escalate privileges, and is urging immediate upgrades.

What’s new New CVE (2026-20316) confirmed under active exploitation as of July 29-30 — a separate FMC hard-coded-credential flaw, distinct from the ongoing Cisco SD-WAN zero-day campaign (UAT-8616) already being tracked.
How outlets frame it Outlets agree on the core technical facts (static credential, KEV listing, mitigation steps); coverage differs mainly in how much weight is put on the “can be chained to escalate privileges” risk versus the more straightforward credential-theft angle.
Context

Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) is the centralized console enterprises use to configure and monitor Cisco firewalls. CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog lists flaws confirmed to be actively used in real attacks, which triggers mandatory-patching deadlines for U.S. federal agencies.
