Samsung and Broadcom strike ~$200B AI chip pact spanning memory, foundry, and packaging

Samsung Electronics and Broadcom signed a five-year memorandum of understanding worth roughly $200 billion covering next-generation HBM4/HBM4E memory, 2-nanometer-and-below foundry manufacturing, and advanced packaging for Broadcom's AI accelerators. The pact, still a statement of intent rather than a binding contract, is meant to secure Samsung high factory utilization while giving Broadcom dedicated capacity for AI and networking silicon. It lands amid warnings of a broader global memory supply crunch and adds a major new foundry customer relationship to watch alongside TSMC's.

What’s new First detailed terms of the MOU circulating this week: HBM4/HBM4E supply, 2nm-and-below foundry work, and packaging, structured as a 5-year framework through 2030.
How outlets frame it Samsung's own release frames the deal as deepening collaboration; The Week's coverage foregrounds the global memory-supply crunch as the deal's backdrop, while trade press stresses the competitive angle against TSMC.
Context

Broadcom, long known for networking chips, has become a major designer of custom AI accelerator chips (ASICs) for hyperscale cloud providers; HBM (high-bandwidth memory) is stacked directly next to AI chips so they can be fed data fast enough for training and inference.

DRC Ebola outbreak grows to ~1,850 deaths, now the largest ever recorded in the country

↪ Follow-up to the thread tracked since Aug 6: cumulative toll and geographic spread have both worsened.

The Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC has climbed to roughly 1,850 confirmed deaths and spread to 53 of the country's 140 health zones across five provinces, per UN reporting. It is now described as the largest Ebola epidemic ever recorded in the DRC and the fastest-growing on record, worsened by weak health infrastructure and ongoing armed conflict in the region. Two vaccine candidates (Moderna's mRNA-1469 and an Oxford/Serum Institute of India shot) have reached human trials, but conclusive results remain months away; a new treatment center has just opened at the outbreak's center.

What’s new Death toll has risen from the ~1,657 reported Aug 1 to roughly 1,850, and the affected-zone count has grown from 49 to 53, per the newest reporting.
Context

The Bundibugyo strain is a less-common Ebola species for which there is no approved vaccine (existing vaccines target the more common Zaire strain), which is part of why this outbreak has proven harder to contain.
