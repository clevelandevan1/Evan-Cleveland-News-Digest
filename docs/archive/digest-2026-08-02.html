CISA urges water utilities to pull exposed PLCs offline after multistate attacks

CISA warned that threat actors are actively targeting internet-exposed programmable logic controllers — mostly Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley, Siemens, and Schneider Electric gear — at water and wastewater utilities in at least seven states, in some cases locking out operators and disrupting service. The alert follows attacks that disrupted more than 30 community water systems in Minnesota and comes amid a similar, separately reported Michigan incident under FBI investigation. CISA is urging operators to remove PLCs and other OT devices from direct internet exposure immediately.

What’s new New alert (July 30) broadens the known pattern beyond the Minnesota/Michigan incidents to at least seven states, and flags cellular modems as a commonly overlooked exposure point.
Context

A PLC (programmable logic controller) is an industrial computer that directly operates physical equipment — pumps, valves, treatment processes — at facilities like water utilities. Exposing one to the open internet lets an attacker manipulate real-world infrastructure directly, not just IT systems.

⚑ Distinct from the ongoing Cisco SD-WAN campaign and squarely in the networking/OT-security space — worth flagging to customers running internet-exposed ICS/OT gear.
