DRC's Ebola outbreak is now the second-largest on record, spreading faster than any prior one

WHO puts the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo at 3,748 cases and 1,657 deaths as of Aug. 1 — a case-fatality picture that has made it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, spreading faster than any previous one. Two rival vaccine efforts are now in human trials: Moderna's mRNA-1469 (first doses given in Canada, authorized by Health Canada) and a University of Oxford/Serum Institute of India candidate, while Africa's CDC is separately planning to trial Merck's existing Ervebo vaccine, which targets a different Ebola species, as a stopgap. Conclusive trial results are still likely months away.

How outlets frame it CNN emphasizes the outbreak's severity and record pace; Bloomberg and Euronews focus on the vaccine-trial response and scientific mechanics; none editorialize on response adequacy.
Context

Bundibugyo ebolavirus is one of several Ebola virus species; it was first identified in Uganda in 2007 and has historically been rarer than the Zaire strain most vaccines (including Merck's Ervebo) were designed against — hence the need for new, strain-specific candidates.
