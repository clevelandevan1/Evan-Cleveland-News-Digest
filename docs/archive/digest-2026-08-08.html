Actively exploited N-able N-central flaw lets attackers take over MSP consoles

CVE-2026-18577 lets an unauthenticated attacker bypass N-central authentication and gain full admin control, then pivot into every endpoint the console manages. N-able's first patch attempt was incomplete — attackers found a bypass path, and CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Aug 3, giving federal agencies a tight patch deadline. Attackers have used the access to open persistent Cloudflare tunnels into victim networks, a pattern now confirmed across multiple customer compromises.

What’s new N-able's initial fix for the underlying bug (CVE-2026-18556) was incomplete; attackers found a new bypass (CVE-2026-18577), prompting CISA's Aug 3 KEV addition and a fresh federal patch deadline.
How outlets frame it BleepingComputer, The Hacker News and SecurityWeek largely agree on the technical mechanics. N-able's own advisory frames the impact as affecting a 'limited number' of customers, while researcher writeups (Rapid7, Huntress) describe broader, ongoing exploitation with many installs still unpatched.
Context

N-central is remote monitoring and management (RMM) software used by managed service providers (MSPs) to administer client networks. A single compromised N-central server can give an attacker a foothold into every business that MSP manages — both on-premises and cloud-hosted deployments are affected.

AMD buys AI chip startup Taalas to challenge Nvidia with model-etched silicon

↪ Part of the ongoing hyperscaler AI-infrastructure buildout thread — a new front (chip specialization) alongside the capex/circular-financing debate tracked since July 29.

AMD is acquiring Toronto startup Taalas, which hardwires a single trained AI model directly into silicon rather than using a general-purpose programmable GPU. In a demo running Llama 3.1 8B, Taalas's chip reportedly hit roughly 17,000 tokens per second — about 48x the throughput of Nvidia's H200/B200 GPUs on the same task. The tradeoff: a model baked into silicon can't be updated on the fly, capping refresh cycles at roughly once every two months even as underlying AI models change weekly.

What’s new Acquisition announced Aug 6 — AMD's first move into model-specific ('etched') inference silicon, a distinct approach from its existing GPU line.
Context

Inference chips run an already-trained AI model to generate outputs cheaply at scale, as distinct from training chips that build the model. Etching a model into silicon trades flexibility for raw speed and efficiency.

⚑ Worth tracking whether AMD offers etched-silicon inference commercially — it could undercut GPU-based inference costs for stable, high-volume enterprise workloads.
