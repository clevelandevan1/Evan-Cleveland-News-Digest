Trump says he'll declare the Strait of Hormuz US 'territory'; Iran calls it a 'delusion'

↪ Follow-up to last Thursday's 'total control'/'wall of steel' claims; Trump has now escalated further, and Iran's rejection is sharper than its prior denials.

Trump said that once the war against Iran is "finished," he plans to declare the Strait of Hormuz "a territory of the United States," pointing to the nearly six-month US naval blockade that has cut commercial traffic through the waterway to a trickle. The Strait of Hormuz is a roughly 21-mile-wide waterway between Iran and Oman through which about a fifth of global oil supply normally passes. A US naval blockade tied to the broader US-Israel war on Iran, in place since spring, has sharply reduced traffic through it.

Coca-Cola confirms data stolen in ransomware attack on Fairlife dairy unit

Coca-Cola confirmed the Anubis ransomware group stole data from its Fairlife dairy subsidiary in an attack disclosed in mid-July that disrupted production at Fairlife's US facilities. Anubis claims to have taken roughly 1 terabyte of data and published it on its leak site after a ransom deadline passed unpaid on July 27. Coca-Cola says a majority of production at Fairlife's four US facilities has since resumed.

What’s new New confirmation: Coca-Cola has now officially confirmed the data theft itself (beyond the production disruption reported in July), and Anubis has followed through on publishing the stolen dataset.
Context

Anubis is a ransomware-as-a-service group that both encrypts victims' systems and steals their data, then threatens to leak it if a ransom isn't paid — the 'double extortion' model now standard among major ransomware operators.
